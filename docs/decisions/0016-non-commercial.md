# 0016. A non-commercial project: nothing is sold, and the Ministry maintains Tabachir after adoption

- **Date:** 2026-09-29, amended 2026-09-30
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Decided by:** the lead maintainer, after at least 30 days of comments, because this changes principle 4 and the process for changing a principle

## Context

On 29 September 2026 the founder set the project's goal. Tabachir is not a commercial venture. It is an open-source project whose primary objective is national adoption, with the Ministry running it on government servers.

The money rules were written for another model:
- principle 4 reads "Charge for services, never for features" ([0006](0006-money-services-not-features.md));
- [0014](0014-business-model-and-sync-pricing.md) priced sync after the pilot, and planned paid deployment, training and support for institutions, with a public price list;
- PRD §9 is a business section, and it recommends a company as the legal entity.

Decision record 0015, proposed alongside this one, makes sync free and has the Ministry run the national system itself. What is left of the old model is selling services to schools and authorities. That would make the project a supplier to the Ministry, with a stake in the Ministry depending on it. The goal is the opposite: a Ministry that runs Tabachir without the project.

Teachers also fear that a free tool will start charging once their records are in it, and they have seen teacher apps vanish (research 04, 05).

After adoption, the Ministry's staff would maintain the code as well as run the servers, while the principles and the charter are still decided in the project's public process. Without a rule on who approves a change, the teachers' employer could help rewrite the protections written for them.

On 30 September, before any comments, the founder settled which sponsors the project refuses, that no money comes from abroad, and who approves a change to the principles or the charter after adoption. This record was amended to match.

## Options

- **Keep principle 4,** and charge for services such as deployments, training and support. It could fund a team, but it makes the project a supplier to the state it wants to hand the system to.
- **Let public bodies cover the project's costs** through published grants or conventions, never as a sale. It keeps a funded team after adoption, but the project and the Ministry would then depend on each other's budgets.
- **Sell nothing, and let the Ministry maintain Tabachir after adoption.** Chosen.

## Decision

- **Principle 4 becomes "Free, and never for sale":**

  > Everything Tabachir does is free, for teachers and for the state. The project sells nothing: no features, services, support or data. Until the Ministry adopts Tabachir, it is funded by public grants and prizes, teachers' donations and sponsors who get no access to data. After adoption, the Ministry maintains Tabachir with its own staff, in the project's public process (§1.10).

- **Teachers never pay.** Every feature is free, and so are sync and every export. Donations are voluntary and unlock nothing.
- **Nobody else pays the project either.** It takes no payment from the Ministry, directorates or schools for Tabachir or for help with it. The deployment guide, the administration manual and the training material are published free, so the Ministry can do the work itself.
- **Funding until adoption:**
  - public grants and prizes that are open to any project, such as INRE's Tarbya-Up Challenge;
  - teachers' donations;
  - sponsors who get no access to data.

  Every source is disclosed, and every income and cost appears in the transparency report (principle 8).
- **No money from abroad.** No foreign grants, prizes, funds or sponsors, and donations only through channels approved in Algeria. Algerian law restricts foreign funding for associations, and the Ministry must be able to trust that no foreign funder has a hold on the project. Counsel confirms the rules.
- **Sponsors** get no data, no say in the roadmap and no place in the app. They are thanked on the website and in the transparency report.
  - The project refuses sponsors that sell to schools, teachers, pupils or parents, such as publishers, tutoring firms, EdTech firms and private schools, and any party, union or religious body.
  - No sponsor gives more than a quarter of the project's income in any year.
- **After adoption, the Ministry maintains Tabachir** with its own staff, who join the project's public process as maintainers (0015).
  - From then on, a change to the principles or the charter passes only with the consent of the teacher council (PRD §1.8), after its 30 days of comments. The Ministry's maintainers can propose a change, but never decide one alone.
  - Principles 2 and 3 stay permanent.
- **Still never:** ads, trackers, selling or sharing data, paid features, or charging anyone to get their own data out (principle 3).
- **The legal entity** is an association, which counsel confirms, not a company.
- **No promise beyond the principles.** The charter carries no "free forever" promise, and there is no committed support channel. Volunteers give support as they can.

This record replaces 0006, and what 0015 leaves of 0014.

Details: PRD §1.10 and §9, rewritten once this is accepted.

## Consequences

- **Principle 4 changes, and so does how a principle changes after adoption.** Principles 2 and 3 are permanent and untouched. The record was amended on 30 September, before any comments, so its 30 days of comments restart (PRD §1.2).
- **Until adoption, the project's capacity depends on volunteers and grants.** Money goes first to plan-pack curation, then to the independent security review and to hosting in Algeria.
- **Less money, and nobody with a hold.** Without money from abroad, conflicted sponsors or any sponsor above a quarter of the income, the project may raise less before adoption. In return, no funder can be seen to steer a record the state may adopt.
- **Teachers keep the last word on their protections.** After adoption, the charter and the principles can't change without the teacher council. `GOVERNANCE.md`, written for the launch, sets how the council gives its consent. The Ministry can still change its own build, as the licence allows, and 0015 then has the project publish the finding at once.
- **The Ministry needs its own team to maintain Tabachir** after adoption. The ask to the Ministry says so, and the project documents everything that team needs, from building a release to restoring a backup.
- **With nothing sold,** the project needs no commercial register entry, and the e-commerce rules of Loi 18-05 no longer apply. Counsel checks the rules for an association that receives donations and grants.
- **Donations start once the association exists** and has an account, through channels approved in Algeria.
- **The rest of the PRD is brought in line:** §1.2, §1.4, §1.8, §1.10, §7.9, §9 and §10.
