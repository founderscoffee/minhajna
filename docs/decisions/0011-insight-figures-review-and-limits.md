# 0011. Insight figures: the IGP's review and the limits on use

- **Date:** 2026-09-27
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decided by:** the founder

## Context

PRD §1.6 left open whether the IGP must agree before figures from the opt-in insights go public, or only sees them first. Two risks shape the answer:
- **Figures used against the people who report them.** Progress figures have been used to set the scope of exams ("thresholds"). When figures shrink an exam's scope, teachers gain by reporting less; when they feed pay or careers, teachers gain by reporting more. Either way the figures stop being true.
- **A school with a single teacher of a subject.** Removing names doesn't protect that teacher. A group size that counts only pupils would still point to them.

## Options

- **The IGP must agree before publication.** It is closer to the state, but the IGP could hold figures back.
- **No special role for the IGP.** It is simple, but it gives the inspectorate no chance to comment.
- **The IGP sees each report first, with a fixed review period.** Chosen.

## Decision

- **The review.** The IGP receives each report first and has 30 days to comment. Publication then follows the method published in advance.
- **What the figures describe.** The curriculum plan, not classes or teachers.
- **What they are never used for:** setting exam scope, ranking anyone, or personnel decisions.
- **Minimum group sizes** count teachers and schools, not only pupils. Cells that would let a hidden figure be worked out are suppressed too.
- **What insights may contain:** what was taught, never when, and never why a session was not held. They never include data from institution mode.

Details: [PRD §1.6](../prd/PRD.md#16-anonymous-insights-rules-for-openness).

## Consequences

- **The insights section** sets the exact minimum group sizes.
- **Counsel** confirms, before the first collection, that teachers may send lesson-level data without written authorisation (Ord. 06-03 Art. 48).
