# 0017. How far classes got may inform the scope of every exam, at the level that sets it

- **Date:** 2026-09-29, amended 2026-09-30, accepted 2026-10-04
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decided by:** the lead maintainer, before the 30 days of comments that a change to the charter calls for had passed. No comments had come in.

## Context

Charter point 7 (PRD §1.15) says that aggregates are never used for exam thresholds or personnel decisions. The charter changes only through the process for changing a principle. [0011](0011-insight-figures-review-and-limits.md) made the same promise for the opt-in insights.

Algeria has set exam "thresholds" from progress collections before, for the baccalaureate in 2014 and 2020. Both came after teaching time was lost, to a long strike in 2014 and to the closures of 2020, and in 2014 the exam itself was postponed. In April 2026, correspondence 50 had CEM directors report the last unit taught in each subject, to adjust the exams (research 08, 09, 11). So the Ministry collects progress data with or without Tabachir, and those collections name each school.

Decision record 0015, proposed alongside this one, lets the national system form totals of how far classes got, without the server learning any school's or teacher's figure. A school's own records stay in its space.

The research also warns that when figures can shrink an exam's scope, a class gains by reporting less (research 11). Such figures stay true only if they are checked.

On 29 September 2026 the founder chose to let wilaya and national totals inform exam scope. Later that day, before any comments, they noted that the threshold serves every kind of exam, not only the BEM and the BAC: a school's term and mock exams, and a directorate's unified and mock exams, also keep their questions within what was taught. So they extended the decision to every exam, each at the level that sets it. On 30 September they settled who sees a cut before the exam, how setters who disagree decide, which figures may shape a cut, what happens when an exam moves or the inspectors' sample is not run, and that a school's cut is optional. This record was amended to match.

## Options

- **Keep the ban,** and offer only the inspectors' sample of exercise books (PRD §8.5). It keeps the promise, but the Ministry is likely to go on collecting progress school by school, by other means.
- **Allow any use of the figures.** Simple, but nothing would check them, and nothing would stop a figure for one class or one teacher from leaving the school.
- **Allow wilaya and national totals only, for national exams.** The first version of this record. It left the school's and the directorate's own exams without figures, so those would go on being collected by other means.
- **Each exam at its own level, under safeguards.** Chosen.

## Decision

- **Charter point 7 becomes:**

  > **Aggregates only above the school.** Minimum sizes and methods are published in advance. Aggregates are never used for personnel decisions. How far classes got may inform the scope of an exam only at the level that sets it: a school's own records for its term and mock exams, a directorate's totals for its exams, and wilaya and national totals for national exams. The figures are taken on a date announced at the start of the school year. It moves only if the exam does, and is then announced again at least two weeks ahead. The figures are checked against the inspectors' sample of pupils' exercise books, or marked unchecked in a year without one, and are published with how they were used once the exam is over.

- **Each exam at its own level:**
  - **A school's term and mock exams,** those with a common paper for parallel classes, use the school's own records, formed in its space. They never leave it. Monthly tests stay with each teacher, with nothing to sign.
    - A school's cut is optional: the subject's teachers use it when they wish. Without one, they set the paper as they do today, nothing is published, and nothing counts which schools went without.
  - **A directorate's unified and mock exams** use its totals.
  - **National exams, such as the BEM and the BAC,** use wilaya and national totals.
  - No figure for one class or one teacher leaves the school. Every total above the school meets the minimum group sizes of [0013](0013-insights-payload-and-minimum-group-sizes.md).
- **Only signed progress above the school.** Totals count what classes completed in signed weeks, which the inspectors' sample checks. Classes' plans stay inside the school: nobody checks them, and a lower plan would pull a cut down.
- **No standing series.** Above the school, totals of how far classes got are formed only on dates announced at the start of the year: the end of each term and each exam's own date. Only the figures from an exam's own date shape its cut.
- **The system shows, people decide.** On the announced date, the system shows the share of classes that completed each lesson, and marks the last lesson taught in common. The exam's setters choose the cut and sign it: the school's teachers of the subject, the directorate's committee, or the Ministry's committee with inspectors. The system never sets a cut by itself.
- **When the setters disagree:** at a school, the earliest proposed cut applies, so no class is examined on what it did not reach. In a committee, the majority decides, so one member cannot narrow an exam alone.
- **The three safeguards, at every level:**
  1. **A date set in advance.** Each exam's figures are taken on a date announced at the start of the school year, in the calendar of the level that sets the exam, never through a sudden collection. If the exam's date moves, the figures' date moves with it, announced again at least two weeks ahead, and the published page shows both dates and why.
  2. **Published after the exam.** Once the exam is over, the cut, its date, the figures used and how they shaped the exam are published: a school's within its space, since its records never leave it, and a directorate's or the Ministry's to the public. Before the exam, the cut stays with its setters and those who organise the exam: the director for a school's exams, the directorate's exams office, and the body that runs a national exam.
  3. **Checked.** Inspectors compare pupils' exercise books with the texts book in a random sample of classes, twice a year (PRD §8.5). The results are published with the figures. In a year without the sample, the figures are still used, and the published page says they were not checked. Refusing them would push the Ministry back to collections that name each school.
- **Teachers see what the director sees.** Each teacher sees where their classes stand against each exam's date, the school's chart for their subject and level, and the directorate's totals, exactly as the director and the directorate see them. They see a cut when they sign it as a setter, or when it is published.
- **What the national system enforces.** It forms each exam's figures only on the announced date, keeps each signed cut with the figures it came from, and publishes them after the exam, so no unpublished figure from it can be used. The Ministry is asked to adopt all three safeguards in its text, as a request, not a condition (0015).
- **The opt-in insights keep their ban** (0011). Figures from teachers who opted in are never used for exam scope.

## Consequences

- **Only charter point 7 changes.** The change stays open for at least 30 days of comments (PRD §1.2). The record was amended on 30 September, the day after it was proposed and before any comments, so its 30 days restart: comments stay open until at least 30 October 2026.
- **The risk remains, and the safeguards answer it.** If teachers believe that reporting less shrinks an exam, some may. But the figures come from the signed weekly records, which are official and always show corrections, only signed progress counts, the date is fixed in advance, and the inspectors' sample checks the records.
- **Two of the safeguards depend on others.** The sample depends on the IGP, and publishing how the figures were used depends on the Ministry and the directorates. The project builds the system to support both, and asks for them. A year without the sample is published as unchecked, so nobody can present its figures as checked.
- **The threshold becomes a primary metric** on the dashboards of the school, the directorate and the Ministry, with charts of how far classes got.
- **The rest of the PRD is brought in line** in a separate pull request: §1.6, §2.6 and §8.4 keep the ban for the opt-in insights and point to charter point 7 for exam thresholds, §5.10 forms progress totals above the school only on announced dates, and the dashboards show the threshold first.
